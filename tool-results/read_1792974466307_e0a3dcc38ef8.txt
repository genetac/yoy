   407→  "02-pricing-tables/three-tier-builder.md": `# The three-tier pricing builder
   408→
   409→The system behind every investment table in this pack. Read once, then
   410→build your table in 30 minutes.
   411→
   412→## Why three tiers (and not one, or five)
   413→
   414→- **One price is a yes/no question.** The client's only move is accept
   415→  or negotiate — you have handed them the pen.
   416→- **Three prices is a comparison question.** "Which of these fits?" The
   417→  client compares your options to each other instead of comparing you to
   418→  a cheaper competitor. That comparison happens inside your proposal,
   419→  where the differences are honest and visible.
   420→- **Five options is a research project.** Choice overload slows
   421→  decisions; slow decisions go to procurement.
   422→
   423→The top and bottom tiers are not decoration: the bottom tier makes the
   424→middle look safe, the top tier makes the middle look sensible. Both must
   425→still be options you would happily deliver.
   426→
   427→## The build order (do not skip steps)
   428→
   429→1. **Define the outcome all three tiers deliver.** Write one sentence:
   430→   "Every option achieves {{outcome}}; they differ in depth and speed."
   431→   If a tier cannot achieve the outcome, it is a Strawman — cut it.
   432→2. **Build the middle tier first.** This is the work you actually want
   433→   to do at a price you actually want to charge. It gets your best
   434→   thinking.
   435→3. **Carve the bottom tier from it.** Same quality, less depth: fewer
   436→   deliverables, longer timeline, thinner support. Never worse craft.
   437→4. **Extend the top tier from it.** More of what worked: faster
   438→   timeline, extra deliverables, deeper support, priority access.
   439→5. **Set the ladder.** A ratio that reads honestly: middle ≈ 1.8–2.2×
   440→   the bottom, top ≈ 3–4× the bottom. Round to clean numbers.
   441→6. **Name and mark.** Plain names (see below), and mark the middle tier
   442→   **recommended** in the table itself.
   443→
   444→## The tier definition grid
   445→
   446→Fill every cell — empty cells are where negotiations start:
   447→
   448→| Row | Bottom | Middle — recommended | Top |
   449→| --- | --- | --- | --- |
   450→| Name | {{tier_a_name}} | {{tier_b_name}} | {{tier_c_name}} |
   451→| Price | {{tier_a_price}} | {{tier_b_price}} | {{tier_c_price}} |
   452→| Duration | {{tier_a_weeks}} | {{tier_b_weeks}} | {{tier_c_weeks}} |
   453→| Deliverables | {{tier_a_list}} | {{tier_b_list}} | {{tier_c_list}} |
   454→| Revisions | 1 round | 2 rounds | 2 rounds + {{tier_c_extra}} |
   455→| Support after delivery | {{tier_a_support}} | {{tier_b_support}} | {{tier_c_support}} |
   456→| Best for | {{tier_a_best_for}} | {{tier_b_best_for}} | {{tier_c_best_for}} |
   457→
   458→**Names:** functional beats clever. "Essential / Recommended /
   459→Complete" outperforms invented names because the client can rank them
   460→without you in the room.
   461→
   462→## Which tier to mark recommended
   463→
   464→The one that hits the client's stated outcome with the least waste. Two
   465→rules:
   466→
   467→1. **Never recommend the top tier by default.** It reads as upsell and
   468→   contaminates the trust the table built.
   469→2. **Never recommend a tier the client's own words don't support.**
   470→   Their section-2 quotes tell you their priority: speed, depth, or
   471→   price. The recommendation must match that priority — that is why the
   472→   proposals in this pack quote the client before the pricing table.
   473→
   474→## When NOT to use three tiers
   475→
   476→- **Small engagements** where the table outweighs the work: quote one
   477→  price, in a paragraph.
   478→- **Repeat clients** who already know your quality: offer one option
   479→  and one alternative, in prose.
   480→- **Procurement-led buys** that will re-cut anything you present: give
   481→  your itemised rate basis and let them build the tier.
   482→
   483→## The three mistakes that break the table
   484→
   485→1. **The strawman bottom.** A tier designed to lose. Clients spot it,
   486→   and the trust cost lands on the middle tier you wanted to win.
   487→2. **Six differences between tiers.** If every row differs, nobody can
   488→   compare. Keep the differences to 3–4 visible rows.
   489→3. **Padding with junk lines.** "Priority email support" that was free
   490→   last year. Every differentiator must cost you something real or the
   491→   tier is dishonest.
